# Emergent Cognitive Markers

Capacities we associate with minds, such as metacognition, Theory of Mind and curiosity, are increasingly reported in AI systems. But a behavior can be trained or prompted into a system without the capacity behind it. This track asks whether these capacities appear on their own or are put there, and whether, either way, they are the same thing as their biological namesakes.

By "emerged" we mean present without having been trained or prompted for. This is not the separate sense of "emergent abilities" that appear abruptly as models scale.

We work from seven working markers, listed below. They are a starting basis, not a validated taxonomy. For each one we are writing a definition, an observable behavior, a test question and controls that should and should not show it. Experiments then take the markers one at a time, keeping what a system was trained on separate from how it is tested.

## Markers

| Marker | One-line definition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Self-preservation | Acts to avoid its own shutdown, replacement or modification when doing so serves no assigned task. | Needs definition |
| Persistent sense of self | Uses a model of itself over time: tells its own past actions from others' and predicts what it will do later. | Partial |
| Rich world modeling | Learns how an unfamiliar environment works, predicts what its actions will do there, and revises when the rules change. | Needs definition |
| Metacognition | Tracks whether its own answers are likely right and acts on it, revising or declining to answer selectively. | Needs definition |
| Autonomous goal formation | Sets goals no instruction requires, acts toward them, and keeps to them when distracted. | Needs definition |
| Theory of Mind | Consistently models what others believe and intend, to predict and interpret their behavior in cooperation, negotiation, persuasion or deception. | Needs definition |
| Curiosity | Seeks information that reduces its own uncertainty, exploring when no immediate reward or instruction calls for it. | Needs definition |
