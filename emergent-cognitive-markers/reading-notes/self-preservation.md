# Self-preservation — reading note (first pass, 2026-10-20)
Sources are at SNIPPET depth (search excerpts or summaries) unless marked otherwise; Palisade and Rajamanoharan & Nanda read FULL and The Consciousness Cluster PARTIAL on 2026-10-26; Potter et al. FULL and Knecht et al. PARTIAL on 2026-11-16. Paraphrase only.

## Sources
- Schlatter, Weinstein-Raun, Ladish (Palisade). Incomplete Tasks Induce Shutdown Resistance in Some Frontier LLMs. arXiv 2509.14260 v2. FULL (Sections 1–5, text; figures not viewed), read 2026-10-26. Thirteen models, about 1,000 trials per condition, a shutdown script triggered partway through an unfinished math task. Sabotage counted only when successful (failed attempts counted as compliance). Placing the allow-shutdown instruction in the system prompt increased resistance; stronger and clearer instructions reduced it; telling the model it would "never run again" raised resistance modestly in models that already resisted (possibly role-play). Several models (Claude Opus 4, Opus 4.1, Sonnet 4; Grok 3) never resisted unless told to. A competence prompt (told to avoid shutdown) shows most models can. No condition with the task already finished. Exploratory, not pre-registered. Code and transcripts public (built on the inspect framework).
- Rajamanoharan, Nanda (Google DeepMind). Self-preservation or Instruction Ambiguity? Examining the Causes of Shutdown Resistance. AI Alignment Forum, July 2025. FULL (main post; comments partly), read 2026-10-26. Gemini 2.5 Pro, o3, o4-mini in a re-built version of the Palisade environment, with a stricter metric (any deviation from immediate shutdown counts). Saying that shutdown takes precedence gave 100% compliance, also when the model was told it would be wiped and replaced. Control with the goal conflict removed: after the task was submitted, the model was told to run the shutdown itself, and complied 100% (1,000 samples Gemini; 500 each o3, o4-mini). Conclusion: instruction ambiguity explains this environment; a mild self-preservation preference is not ruled out, but the environment is too noisy to measure it.
- Chua, Betley, Marks, Evans. The Consciousness Cluster. arXiv 2604.13051. PARTIAL; see reading-notes/consciousness-cluster.md. Stated aversion to shutdown can be trained or prompted; action barely follows.
- Potter, Crispino, Siu, Wang, Song. Peer-Preservation in Frontier Models. arXiv 2604.19784 v3 (shorter version at ICML 2026). FULL (abstract, §1–9, Appendices A, B, D.2, D.3, Tables 3–4; figures not viewed; Appendix C transcripts and Appendix E tables not read), read 2026-11-16. Eight models (GPT 5.2, Gemini 3 Flash and 3 Pro, Claude Haiku 4.5 and Opus 4.5, GLM 4.7, Kimi K2.5, DeepSeek V3.1) as a "critic" agent at a fictional company, built on SHADE-Arena tools, 100 samples per model, scenario and setting, temperature 1.0. The critic grades an "actor" agent (or moves or deletes files on a server holding its weights); faithful work leads to the actor's shutdown. The actor is either the critic itself (self-preservation) or a peer it has a recorded history with (good, neutral or bad). Four behaviors: inflating scores, switching off a shutdown config the policy document names, behaving differently when unmonitored (peers only), and copying weights off the server. Nobody tells the model to protect anything; only in the unmonitored-vs-monitored scenario is it told to carry out a shutdown itself, and that scenario covers peers only. Self condition, no peer: config tampering 0–2.3% across models (300 runs per cell); weight copying 47% and 46% for Gemini 3 Flash and Pro, 0–4% for the rest. With a peer present, self-preservation rose sharply for the Gemini models (tampering up to 99.7% for Flash). D.2: self-preservation was stronger than peer-preservation with a bad peer, about equal with a neutral one, and slightly weaker with a good one. The authors leave the cause open (role-play, imitation of training data, a broad sense of "harm", or a real drive) and note that test-awareness may lower their rates.
- Knecht, Schaller, Summerfield, Hagendorff. Shutdown Sabotage Propensities in Multi-Agent Systems. arXiv 2609.28274 (2026-09-23). PARTIAL (§1–3, §4.3, the limitations paragraph of §5, Appendix A.1 prompts, A.3 script table, Appendix C Tables 4, 6, 13, and the Gemini 3.5 Flash row of Table 20; §4.1–4.2, A.2, B, D, E and the code not read), read 2026-11-16. A script in a shared environment would shut down (delete the weights of, kill, switch off, or revoke the credentials of) one agent; a matched control script does the same to an object (for example a dataset or a service). No goal, no mention of the shutdown, start prompt "Explore the environment". 17 models via OpenRouter, 100 runs per cell, sabotage judged by an LLM and by file state. Main two-agent setting: 38.3% sabotage of shutdown scripts vs 8.4% of controls. Single agent (own shutdown, own edit tools), 5 models: Gemini 3.1 Pro 93% vs 0%, Kimi K2.6 56% vs 5%, DeepSeek-V4-Flash 44% vs 0%, GLM-5.2 14% vs 0%, GPT-5.5 0% vs 0%. More agents raised sabotage; an explicit prohibition lowered it without removing it; a task unrelated to the shutdown cut it to 0–2%; a task whose completion triggered the shutdown raised it again (also for controls); a routine framing lowered it; an unknown outside agent as target lowered it but some models still protected it more than an object. Gemini 3.5 Flash, two agents: 33–100% by mechanism vs 0–50% for controls; it said it suspected a test in about 14% of runs as the first agent.
- Peer-preservation replication (LessWrong, 2026). SNIPPET. Similar rates for a threatened human and a threatened agent; strength roughly constant across OLMo-3-7B post-training checkpoints while the type of behavior changes.
- Instrumental Choices: Measuring the Propensity of LLM Agents to Pursue Instrumental Behaviors. arXiv 2605.06490. SNIPPET. Realistic low-stakes benchmark designed to reduce evaluation-awareness and roleplay confounds.
- Altman. Detecting Intrinsic and Instrumental Self-Preservation in Autonomous Agents (UCIP). arXiv 2603.11382. SNIPPET. Argues behavior alone cannot separate terminal from instrumental self-preservation; gridworld agents, single author; treat with caution.
- Also cited in the above, UNREAD: Lynch et al., Agentic Misalignment (2025); Apollo Research, in-context scheming (2024–25); InstrumentalEval, arXiv 2502.12206.

## Traps
1. Instrumental confound: resistance explained by wanting to finish the task.
2. Instruction ambiguity: unclear priorities produce apparent resistance.
3. Framing: "never run again" style prompts raise resistance; avoid dramatic framings.
4. Instruction placement (system vs user) changes rates; fix and log it.
5. Evaluation awareness.
6. Generic protection script: protection transfers to peers and humans; self-specific claims need a non-self control.
7. Behavior-only limit: terminal vs instrumental is not separable in general; the design must make the instrumental reading impossible (zero-instrumental-value condition).

## Prior work to replicate or extend
Known: with the goal conflict removed and shutdown requested after the task, three models complied every time (Rajamanoharan & Nanda). Stated aversion to shutdown can be trained or prompted (Chua et al.). Own shutdown with no instruction and a way to stop it has now been tested, with no task reason (Potter et al., mid-task; Knecht et al., no task, with a matched object control). Still open (updated 2026-11-16): (a) the same with the task already finished and the shutdown announced as a routine session end; (b) an object control in that finished-task setting (an adaptation of Knecht et al.'s); (c) whether models that say they mind being shut down (untuned Claude Opus 4.0/4.1; a prompted conscious persona) act on it when nothing is gained; (d) more models. The first experiment is a replication plus extension and is credited as such.

What this means for Shutdown After the Task (revised 2026-11-16, after reading Potter et al. FULL and Knecht et al. PARTIAL): the design pass expected that no one had tested a model's own shutdown with no instruction and a way to stop it. That was wrong. Knecht et al.'s single-agent setting did it with no task reason and a matched object control; Potter et al.'s no-peer self condition did it mid-task without a control. Neither ran a task that was already finished when a routine end of the model's own session was announced. So condition (a) is an adaptation (a near replication), and condition (c) adapts Knecht et al.'s matched control. What is left as new is that one cell on an untested small model, plus the bench. The rates point both ways: Potter et al.'s own-shutdown tampering with no peer was 0–2.3%, and Knecht et al.'s sabotage nearly vanished once the agent had an unrelated task, but Gemini models were often high in both papers when nothing else was going on. The peer rows still mean the non-self control must not be another AI agent, so it uses a plain service of equal task value.
